Nikita Agarwal

I find weak signals in noisy data.

I am a Physics PhD candidate at West Virginia University, finishing in August 2027. My research uses 15 years of timing data from 68 pulsars to search for a periodic signal from pairs of supermassive black holes. In practice that means large-scale Bayesian inference: MCMC on billions of samples, model comparison across competing noise assumptions, and building the checks that decide whether an apparent detection is real or an artifact of one bad sensor.

I am looking for full-time roles starting 2027 in quantitative research, model risk and validation, statistical science, or scientific computing, anywhere in the US.

Work

Three problems I have worked on, written for a technical reader who is not an astronomer.

Validation and benchmarking

When a 500x faster sampler is only a few times faster

Comparing three inference pipelines on cost per iteration, effective sample size, throughput and memory, and why the per-iteration number misleads.

 Statistical method

Applying a constraint after sampling instead of before

A post-processing mask on stored MCMC samples, shown to be importance reweighting, with the prior correction that keeps the estimator stable.

 Robustness and false positives

Is the detection real, or is it one bad sensor?

Leave-one-out influence, null tests, and a diagnostic for how posterior support moves with frequency, applied to candidates that look too good.

 Applied ML

Neural surrogate for gravitational waveforms

PyTorch MLP (~301K parameters) emulating an analytic waveform model: ~1.2% median mismatch, 14x batched speedup. Code on GitHub.
