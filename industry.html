Why this transfers

Pulsar timing is an unusual domain. The statistics underneath it are not. Here is the PhD, line by line, in the vocabulary of the teams I want to join. Click any row for the specifics.

The whole field is one problem: a signal far below the noise of any single detector, correlated noise that imitates it, and a decision about whether the evidence is real. That is also fraud detection, model risk, A/B testing at low effect sizes, and anomaly detection in sensor networks.

What I did

Searched for one faint periodic signal across 68 pulsars over 15 years

What it is called elsewhere

Weak-signal detection in multi-source time series

Each pulsar is a noisy channel with its own noise model. The signal is only visible when all channels are modeled jointly and their noise is separated from the common signal. The same structure appears in multi-sensor monitoring, portfolio-level risk, and anomaly detection where no single stream is conclusive.

What I did

Designed the validation and benchmarking protocol for three competing inference pipelines

What it is called elsewhere

Model validation and benchmarking

Defined the metrics (cost per iteration, effective sample size, throughput of usable samples, peak memory), ran the same simulated problem through each pipeline, and showed that a 500x per-iteration speedup collapses to a factor of a few in independent samples once autocorrelation is accounted for. Results agreed within 0.05 dex. This is the CQG paper. Read the write-up.

What I did

Applied a physical constraint after sampling and proved it was importance reweighting

What it is called elsewhere

Reusing expensive model runs instead of re-running them

Stored samples were reweighted to a new prior in seconds instead of days, with a derived prior floor that keeps the estimator stable and a block bootstrap for uncertainty on correlated samples. The same idea lets a team update a fitted model under a new constraint or scenario without paying the full compute cost again. Read the write-up.

What it is called elsewhere

False-positive control, influence analysis, permutation tests

Before a claim goes out, it has to survive removing the most influential data source, and it has to vanish when the data are scrambled in ways that destroy the real correlation but preserve the noise. This is the discipline behind robust A/B decisions and model-risk sign-off. Read the write-up.
